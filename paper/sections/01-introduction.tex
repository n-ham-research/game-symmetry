\section{Introduction}\label{sec:01-introduction}
Symmetry in a strategic-form game should transport both a player's action
and the payoff recipient consistently. With three or more players, replacing
inverse profile indices by forward indices is not an innocent notational
change: the resulting formula also forces the players' payoff functions to
be identical. The correction in Theorem~\ref{thm:indexing} is established
work of Vester and Ham. Its significance is the exact boundary it places
between ordinary symmetry and common interest, not a failure of equilibrium
existence in every game called symmetric.

There is a second distinction even after the indexing is correct. Every
player permutation may admit strategy relabelings without those realizations
being selectable coherently, and a coherent action may still act nontrivially
on a fixed player's strategies. We develop these requirements in one typed
framework. The restricted assignment and recommendation consequences in
Section~\ref{sec:consequences} explain why none is automatically a universal
fairness criterion.

Ham's binary three-player classification, particularly Figure 4 and
Example 5.13 of \citet{ham2021}, already separates full player image and standard
symmetry from full matching symmetry. Its signed action has a coherent
section with a nontrivial fixed-player strategy action. The present question
is different: can the player projection fail to have \emph{any} section?
Figure 4 is therefore the established starting point, including the
specializations that acquire extra symmetry, rather than a classification
to be rediscovered. We extend it to ternary mechanisms and to the existence
of coherent lifts at arbitrary player counts. Six is the only player count
for standard ternary nonsplitting, and an explicit game attains it.

Throughout we use the inverse-index convention
$(P_p s)_j=s_{p^{-1}(j)}$ and compose maps right to left. This separates the
permutation of recipients from the indexing of profiles. Background on these
conventions and forms of symmetry is available in
\citet{ham2021,vester2012,plan2017,plan2023}. Historical source and version
qualifications are retained in the accompanying publication assessment.

Typed automorphisms and player-image quotients are established constructions.
The covering-group method characterizing which orbit constraints can be full
symmetry groups is also prior work \citep{cao2024}. Likewise the nonsplit
triple cover and its degree 18 representation are known
\citep{gillespie2018,atlas}. Our candidate contributions concern their exact
realization by payoff data, standardness, classification and sharp boundaries.
Fairness and utility-representation questions are developed in a companion
project; the binary equilibrium programme remains within the structural
research programme but is not required for the central lifting argument.

After the indexing correction, the structural extension results are Theorem~\ref{thm:ternary}, which classifies the
three-player ternary case, and Theorem~\ref{thm:ternary-player-counts}, which
restricts nonsplitting to six players under the stated hypotheses.
Theorem~\ref{thm:six} gives the attaining game. We first develop the matching
and orbit-closure tools, review the binary benchmark, and then prove the
classification and player-count restrictions. The explicit construction
makes the distinction between a preserving subgroup and the complete game
group testable on a small payoff table. Appendix~\ref{app:verification}
describes precisely which finite assertions require computation.
