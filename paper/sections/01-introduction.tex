\section{Introduction}
Symmetry expresses invariance of a game's structure under changes of player
positions. Equality of realized payoffs is a different condition. For example,
a symmetric bimatrix game can assign payoffs $(0,5)$ at a profile and $(5,0)$
after exchanging its players and their strategies. The invariance concerns the
relationship between these profiles.

Ham's survey \citep{ham2021} distinguishes label-dependent symmetry from
symmetry that allows strategy relabeling. This project revisits that
classification and its proposed interpretations of fairness. Our initial task
is to check the definitions and proofs before extending the framework.
The words \emph{fair} and \emph{symmetric} will not be used interchangeably
without a specified mathematical condition.

A historical warning motivates the choice of action convention. The condition
attributed to Definition~7 of Dasgupta and Maskin \citep{dm1986} combines a player
permutation with the inverse of the appropriate profile action. Vester's
Appendix~B \citep{vester2012} and Ham's Theorem~3.21 \citep{ham2021} establish its
common-payoff consequence. Ham's first arXiv version already contains this
result as Theorem~3.18 \citep{ham2013}. We present the equivalence with a shorter
proof, without claiming novelty. The effects on the original discontinuous-game
existence arguments require a separate audit and are not asserted here.


Ham's honours thesis, dated 2011, explicitly identifies the mismatch in
Subsection~5.8 \citep{hamthesis}; that thesis was posted to arXiv in 2019.
The Wikipedia record confirms an edit introducing inverse indices on 4 May
2011 and its reversal on 8 May \citep{wiki2011a,wiki2011b}. Ham attributes
the earlier edit to himself \citep{ham2021}; the revision record alone does
not establish the anonymous editor's identity. These records distinguish an
earlier identification from the dates of publicly available proofs.

Von Neumann and Morgenstern's Section~28 studies invariance groups in the
zero-sum n-person setting. Footnote~4 on p.256, continuing onto p.257,
explains why the placement of permutation indices is needed for composition
\citep{vnm1953}. Their fairness condition in Subsection~28.2.2 concerns a
coalition characteristic function depending only on coalition size, rather
than equality of realized payoffs. The first-edition comparison remains
pending. Plan's inspected 2017 draft \citep{plan2017} cites the warning;
it must be distinguished from the final journal article \citep{plan2023},
whose full version comparison remains part of the source audit.
