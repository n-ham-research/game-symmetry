\section{Introduction}
Player labels can be interchangeable in several mathematically distinct
senses. Each permutation may admit suitable strategy relabelings; these
relabelings may be selectable coherently as a group action; and that action
may act trivially on a fixed player's strategies. The last condition identifies
strategies across roles through a matching. Confusing these requirements
obscures both examples and classification statements.

Ham's binary three-player classification, particularly Figure 4 and
Example 5.13 of \citet{ham2021}, already separates full player image and standard
symmetry from full matching symmetry. Its signed action has a coherent
section with a nontrivial fixed-player strategy action. The present question
is different: can the player projection fail to have \emph{any} section?
We prove small-strategy restrictions on that failure and give an explicit
minimal ternary example.

The distinction between coordinate permutations and their inverse profile
indices has a longer history. Von Neumann and Morgenstern's third edition,
\S 28, explicitly discusses the composition convention \citep{vnm1953}; the
1944 first-edition body has not been verified here. Ham's 2011-dated honours
thesis identifies the permutation mismatch associated with the
Dasgupta--Maskin definition \citep{dm1986,hamthesis}. Public Wikipedia revisions
record an inverse-index correction on 4 May 2011 and its reversion on 8 May
\citep{wiki2011a,wiki2011b}; attributing the anonymous correction to Ham rests
on his subsequent account, rather than the revision identity alone.
Vester's 2012 report independently supplies the common-payoff characterization
\citep{vester2012}; Plan's inspected 2017 draft discusses this history
\citep{plan2017}. We distinguish that draft from the 2023 published article
\citep{plan2023}. These historical matters motivate precise definitions; they
are not premises of the lifting theorems.

Typed automorphisms and player-image quotients are established constructions.
The covering-group method characterizing which orbit constraints can be full
symmetry groups is also prior work \citep{cao2024}. Likewise the nonsplit
triple cover and its degree 18 representation are known
\citep{gillespie2018,atlas}. Our candidate contributions concern their exact
realization by payoff data, standardness, classification and sharp boundaries.
Fairness and utility-representation questions are developed in a companion
project; the binary equilibrium programme remains within the structural
research programme but is not required for the central lifting argument.
