\section{A six-player ternary game with binary payoffs}\label{sec:six}
All six strategy sets are $\mathbb F_3$. Let $C$ consist of the 18 words obtained
by adding a common constant to each of the following rows:
\[
\begin{array}{rrrrrr}
0&0&0&1&0&0\\0&0&2&2&2&1\\0&1&1&1&2&2\\
0&1&2&0&1&0\\0&2&0&2&1&2\\0&2&1&0&0&1
\end{array}.
\]
Give every player utility $u_i(s)=1_C(s)$. This definition specifies all 729
profiles without generic utility parameters.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:six}
The complete exact automorphism group $G$ has order 2160, full player image
$S_6$, and kernel the three common translations. The game is standard, but
$q:G\to S_6$ has no section. It consequently has no full matching.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every local ternary permutation is affine $x\mapsto a_i x+b_i$ with
$a_i\in\{1,-1\}$. Typed maps preserve Hamming distance. Comparing the six
rows and their translates shows all distances between distinct codewords
are 4 or 6, and the distance-six triples are precisely
$\{r,r+1,r+2\}$. There are 135 pairs of distance 4 and 18 of distance 6.

An automorphism fixing players permutes these triples. Images of $r,r+1,r+2$
have common increments, so all slopes $a_i$ coincide, say $a$. Modulo common
translation, the first row $r_0=000100$ maps to one of the six displayed rows $r$,
forcing $b=r-ar_0$. There are twelve candidates. Table~\ref{tab:kernel} gives rejection witnesses: codewords whose images lie outside $C$.
\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Rejection witnesses for pointwise-player kernel candidates.}\label{tab:kernel}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\toprule
$r$&$a=1$&$a=-1$\\\midrule
000100&identity&002221\\002221&002221&011122\\
011122&002221&002221\\012010&002221&002221\\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
Thus the actual pointwise-player kernel is exactly the common $C_3$.

The following two typed maps preserve the code, as direct substitution in its
18 words verifies. Maps below are written as image tuples, not cycle notation:
\[
\begin{split}
a:&\ p=(0,3,2,1,4,5),\quad
 f=(021,021,102,021,021,102),\\
b:&\ p=(1,4,0,3,5,2),\quad f_i=012\ \text{for all }i.
\end{split}
\]
Their player permutations are $(1\ 3)$ and $(0\ 1\ 4\ 5\ 2)$, respectively.
Conjugating the transposition by the five-cycle supplies all transpositions
joining 3 to another player, hence generates $S_6$. Orbit--stabilizer for $q$
now gives $|G|=720\cdot3=2160$; this establishes the \emph{complete} group,
including every possible extra binary-payoff automorphism.

For the remaining two finite group facts we use the explicit lemma below.
The two code-preserving maps $x,y$ there generate a transitive subgroup of
order 60 with 60 player images. Its local maps are translations. A point
stabilizer has order 10, so its homomorphism into local $C_3$ is trivial.
This proves strategy triviality and standardness.

The even-player inverse image $H=q^{-1}(A_6)$ has order 1080 and is perfect by
the same lemma. If a section existed, its image would intersect $H$ in a
subgroup of order 360, hence index 3. The action on the three cosets would
give a transitive image of the perfect group $H$ in $S_3$. Images of perfect
groups are perfect, while $S_3$ has no nontrivial perfect subgroup. This is
impossible. Therefore no section exists.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Explicit finite group facts]\label{lem:sixcert}
The maps $a,b$ above generate 2160 elements. The following maps lie in their
group and generate 60 elements with a transitive player image of order 60:
\[
\begin{split}
x:&\ p=(1,0,2,4,3,5),\quad b_x=(1,2,0,1,2,0),\\
y:&\ p=(1,3,5,0,2,4),\quad b_y=(1,2,1,0,1,1),
\end{split}
\]
where $f_i(t)=t+(b_x)_i$ or $t+(b_y)_i$. Moreover $\langle b,aba\rangle$
has 1080 elements and is the normal closure in itself of $[b,aba]$.
\end{lemma}
These small finite assertions are part of the proof, not consequences assumed
from a group name. Their complete verification rule is to start with identity,
close each displayed generator set under left multiplication until no new
permutation tuple occurs, and count the elements and distinct player tuples.
For the last assertion conjugate the indicated commutator by every element
of $\langle b,aba\rangle$ and close those conjugates. Every such conjugate is
a commutator, proving perfectness when the two sets agree. Membership of $x,y$
is checked against the first closed set. All calculations use the multiplication
in Section~\ref{sec:02-preliminaries} on 18 typed strategies. The saved certificate gives complete
input data and checks these counts with three implementations, including two
which do not import the first one's group operations. The analytic 12 case
kernel proof is additionally checked against all $6^6=46656$ pointwise maps.
No search of the 33592320 element ambient typed group is needed.

\begin{remark}[Prior group theory]\label{remark:07-six-player-2}
The generated action is the established degree 18 representation of the
nonsplit triple cover $3.S_6$ from the ATLAS \citep{atlas}. Gillespie,
\'O Cath\'ain and Praeger prove nonsplitting in a related Hadamard realization
\citep{gillespie2018}. Neither the group nor that representation is asserted
new. The theorem verifies this exact binary game's full group, standardness
and represented obstruction independently. If one also notes that $C_3$ is
central in $H$, perfectness gives an even shorter alternative: a section would
make $H=C_3\times A_6$, which is not perfect. The coset argument avoids needing
that additional observation.
\end{remark}
