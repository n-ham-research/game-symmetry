\section{Finite verification and source reproducibility}\label{app:verification}
The finite claims in Lemmas~\ref{lem:finite} and~\ref{lem:sixcert} are
parts of the proofs. They concern specified finite permutation sets; no
finite computation is used to infer a statement about unbounded player counts.
The general player-count argument is the separate proof in
Section~\ref{sec:player-counts}.

For Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}, enumerate the six permutations of three letters
lexicographically and the $6^3$ local triples with player image $(12)$.
Conjugate by the order-twelve stabilizer of this player transposition in the
normalizer of the bare three-cycle. Retain the lexicographically least triple
in each orbit, obtaining 32 representatives. Close each generated subgroup
under left multiplication. On the 81 recipient--profile cells compute its
orbits, then test all 1296 ambient typed maps for preservation of every
individual orbit. Enumerate the 36 matchings with the reference-player map
fixed. A section is certified by generators satisfying the displayed
$S_3$ relations; failure of a matching is exhaustive over this finite list.
These instructions determine the input, stopping criterion and tests underlying
Table~\ref{tab:ternary}.

For Lemma~\ref{lem:sixcert}, the displayed maps on eighteen strategy labels
are sufficient input. Close the generators, project to the six player blocks,
and count the distinct projections. To certify perfectness, close the
conjugates of the displayed commutator and compare with the even-player
preimage. The twelve kernel candidates are separately rejected by
Table~\ref{tab:kernel}. This establishes the complete group, rather than
merely a subgroup of its claimed order.

The source distribution includes the existing standard-library certificate
programs and their saved data as ancillary material. From its \texttt{anc}
directory run
\begin{verbatim}
python3 phase5_ternary_audit.py --verify
python3 phase5_ternary_six_crosscheck.py
\end{verbatim}
The first regenerates and compares the 32-case classification certificate;
the second checks the six-player group from a separate implementation.
The accompanying verification manifest identifies the original repository
files by their hashes. These are reproducible internal checks, not external
peer review or independent evidence of originality.
