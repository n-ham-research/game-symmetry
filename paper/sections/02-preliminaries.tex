\section{Typed automorphisms and lifting}
A game has players $N=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$, nonempty finite strategy sets $A_i$,
and utilities $u_i:\prod_j A_j\to\R$. A typed bijection $g=(p;(f_i))$ consists
of $p\in S_n$ and bijections $f_i:A_i\to A_{p(i)}$, acting by
$(gs)_{p(i)}=f_i(s_i)$. We compose right to left. In particular
$(P_p s)_j=s_{p^{-1}(j)}$. The exact automorphism condition is
\[
 u_{p(i)}(gs)=u_i(s)\qquad(i\in N,\ s\in\prod_j A_j).
\]
These maps form $G=\Aut(u)$, with player homomorphism $q:G\to S_n$.
For equal strategy counts $m$ the ambient group is $S_m^n\rtimes S_n$.
Its multiplication is
$(p;f)(r;h)=(pr;(f_{r(i)}h_i)_i)$.

\begin{definition}
The game has \emph{full player image} if $q(G)=S_n$. A subgroup is
\emph{strategy trivial} if every element fixing a player acts identically
on that player's strategies. The game is \emph{standard symmetric} if it
contains a player-transitive strategy-trivial subgroup. A \emph{section}
is a homomorphism $\sigma:S_n\to G$ with $q\sigma=\id$.
\end{definition}
A matching is a family $t_i:B\to A_i$ of bijections. Its lift of $p$ has
components $t_{p(i)}t_i^{-1}$. Full symmetry under a matching means all these
lifts belong to $G$. It is invariant under exact game isomorphism.

\begin{lemma}[Matching characterization, Ham]\label{lem:matching}
A player-transitive strategy-trivial subgroup becomes a group of bare player
permutations after local relabeling. A strategy-trivial section exists if and
only if the game is fully symmetric under a matching.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose player 0 and an element carrying it to each$i$. Its component at 0 is
a map$t_i$. Any two choices agree because their quotient fixes 0 and acts
identically there. Composition gives$f_i t_i=t_{p(i)}$, hence
$f_i=t_{p(i)}t_i^{-1}$. Conversely matching lifts compose coherently and have
identity components at fixed players. A strategy-trivial group has trivial
player kernel, so full player image makes it a section.
\end{proof}
Consequently matching symmetry implies a section, and a section implies full
player image. A section also implies standardness: a lifted$n$-cycle generates
a regular, thus strategy-trivial, cyclic subgroup. For$n=1$ this is immediate.
The converse implications discussed below fail.

The terminology can be summarized without identifying these properties:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{.28\linewidth}p{.61\linewidth}}
\hline
Property & Requirement on the complete group $G$\\\hline
Standard symmetry & A transitive strategy-trivial subgroup of $G$ exists.\\
Full player image & Every $p\in S_n$ has some lift in $G$.\\
Splitting & Those lifts can be chosen as a homomorphism $S_n\to G$.\\
Strategy-trivial splitting & Such a choice acts identically at fixed players.\\
Full symmetry & Full symmetry under a matching; equivalent to the preceding row.\\\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus full symmetry implies splitting, which implies both standard symmetry
and full player image. Standard symmetry alone need not give full player
image. Ham's Example 5.13 separates splitting from full symmetry; our
six-player game separates standard full-image symmetry from splitting.

\begin{lemma}[Prior covering principle]\label{lem:closure}
Let$B$ be an ambient typed subgroup, acting on the cells$(s,i)$ by
$(s,i)\mapsto(gs,p(i))$. Let$\operatorname{cl}(B)$ consist of all typed maps
preserving each individual$B$-orbit. If$B\le\Aut(u)$, then
$\operatorname{cl}(B)\le\Aut(u)$. Distinct utility values on the cell orbits
realize$\operatorname{cl}(B)$ as the complete group.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Invariance means the utility at a cell is constant on each orbit. Orbit
preservers retain those constants. Distinct orbit values make the converse
necessary. Coincident values can only add automorphisms.
\end{proof}
This is the covering-group construction of the existing literature
\citep{cao2024}, not a new general realization method. Its necessity prevents
mistaking an arbitrary preserving subgroup for a possible complete group.
