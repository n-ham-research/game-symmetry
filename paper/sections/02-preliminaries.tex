\section{Preliminaries}
A finite strategic-form game is $G=(I,(A_i)_{i\in I},(u_i)_{i\in I})$, where
$I=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, $n\ge2$, each $A_i$ is finite and nonempty, and
$u_i:\prod_{j\in I}A_j\to\R$. Write $S=\prod_i A_i$.
A mixed profile $x\in\prod_i\Delta(A_i)$ induces the product probability
$\mu_x(s)=\prod_i x_i(s_i)$. A distribution on $S$ need not have this form:
independent mixing and correlated randomization are different domains.

When $A_i=A$ for all $i$, define
\[
 (P_\pi s)_j=s_{\pi^{-1}(j)}\qquad(\pi\in\Sn).
\]
Thus the action chosen at position $i$ moves to position $\pi(i)$.
Composition is right to left: $(\tau\pi)(i)=\tau(\pi(i))$.
Direct substitution gives $P_\tau P_\pi=P_{\tau\pi}$ and
$P_{\pi^{-1}}=P_\pi^{-1}$. In particular,
$P_{(123)}(s_1,s_2,s_3)=(s_3,s_1,s_2)$, whereas
$P_{(123)^{-1}}s=(s_2,s_3,s_1)$.
These conventions agree with Ham's left action \citep[Section~3.1]{ham2021}.
