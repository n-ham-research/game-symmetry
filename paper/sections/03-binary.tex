\section{The binary benchmark}\label{sec:03-binary}
Figure 4 of \citet{ham2021} is a classification of parameterized binary
three-player symmetry families up to isomorphism. It is prior work. The
following individual-game formulation includes every parameter coincidence.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:binary}
Every three-player binary game with full player image and no full matching is
isomorphic to a specialization of Ham's Example 5.13 with $\beta\ne\gamma$.
Its complete group is the signed $S_3$, and it is standard.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write binary flips as $V=\mathbb F_2^3$, put $z=(1,1,1)$, and let $E$ be the
even-weight space. The player kernel $K$ is invariant under coordinate
permutations. If it contains a nonconstant vector, subtracting a transposed
copy gives a coordinate pair, whose conjugates span $E$. Hence
$K=0,\langle z\rangle,E$, or $V$.

For $K=V$, all utilities are constant, and transitivity equates their values.
For $K=E$, each utility depends only on profile parity. If a transposition lift
changes parity, its fixed player's utility is constant, hence so are all
players' utilities by transitivity. Otherwise every transposition lift
preserves parity and equates the swapped players' parity functions. Bare
permutations then preserve the game.

For $K=\langle z\rangle$, each transposition lift squares to identity: its
square is supported only on the two moved coordinates and lies in the
constant line. For adjacent lifts $t,u$, the defect $(tu)^3$ is $1$ or $z$;
multiplying $t$ by the central $z$ toggles it. Thus the $S_3$ presentation gives
a section. For $K=0$, the projection is already an isomorphism.

A complement's player stabilizer $C_2$ acts on its two strategies either
trivially or by interchange, uniformly across players. In the former case
Lemma\ref{lem:matching} applies. In the latter, twist each odd lift by the
common flip $z$ in the ambient group; its stabilizer actions become trivial.
The matching lemma identifies the original action with
$s\mapsto P_p s+\varepsilon(p)z$. If $K=\langle z\rangle$, the flip is itself
an automorphism, so bare lifts exist as well. Thus a non-full game has
trivial kernel and precisely the signed action.

Its cells have four orbits: homogeneous profiles, minority players, and the
two cyclic orientations of majority-player cells. Assign their payoffs
$\alpha,\delta,\beta,\gamma$. These are exactly Example 5.13. If $\beta=\gamma$,
bare permutations preserve it. Otherwise no nonhomogeneous profile has equal
utilities for all players. Every matching profile must have equal utilities,
since its lifts fix the profile and permute players transitively. A binary
matching must therefore be $\{000,111\}$, and its opponent transposition
exchanges $\beta,\gamma$, which is impossible.
\end{proof}
The proposition refines the quantifiers and proof of the published
classification; it does not claim discovery of another binary family.
