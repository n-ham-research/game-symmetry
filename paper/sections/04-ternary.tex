\section{Three-player ternary actions}
Let$W=S_3^3\rtimes S_3$, with$r=((012);\id,\id,\id)$. For a strategy
transposition$h$ write
\[
 H_h=\{(p;(h^{\varepsilon(p)},h^{\varepsilon(p)},h^{\varepsilon(p)})):p\in S_3\},
 \quad L=\{(p;(f,f,f)):\varepsilon(p)=\varepsilon(f)\}.
\]
\begin{lemma}[Finite classification lemma]\label{lem:finite}
For every odd-player$g\in W$, the group$B=\langle r,g\rangle$ has a section.
Either$\operatorname{cl}(B)$ contains a full matching, or$B=H_h$ for a
strategy transposition$h$. Each$H_h$ is orbit closed and non-full.
\end{lemma}
The following specifies the essential finite part, rather than treating a
passing test suite as a mathematical explanation. The normalizer of
$\langle r\rangle$ is
$\{(p;(f,f,f)):p,f\in S_3\}$, of order 36. Its bare player permutations put
$q(g)=(12)$. There are 216 local triples; simultaneous strategy conjugation and
interchange of the two moved coordinates reduce them to 32 classes. Burnside's
count is$(294+90)/12=32$: the two sums are respectively
$\sum_f|C(f)|^3$ and$\sum_f|C(f)||C(f^2)|$.

groups by normalizer conjugacy. The complete output is:

Every row has section witnesses$a,t$ with player images$(012),(12)$ and
$a^3=t^2=1$, $tat=a^{-1}$. All closures contain a full matching except class 1,
whose three conjugates are$H_h$. The checker enumerates all 36 matchings by
fixing its reference-player bijection and choosing the other two. It computes
orbits on 81 cells and checks every ambient map against every orbit. Positive
word closure terminates and includes inverses because the generators have
finite order. The supplementary verification guide gives the representative
generators, integer encodings, full witnesses and independent implementation.
The table remains an exhaustive finite lemma, not a symbolic proof of its
entries. It replaces the larger 648 lift presentation with a complete 32 case
domain.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:ternary}
Every standard three-player ternary game with full player image has a section.
If it has no full matching, its complete group, up to local labels, is$H_h$
or$L$, of orders 6 and 18. Both occur.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The matching lemma normalizes the standard subgroup to bare permutations.
Every transitive subgroup of$S_3$ contains its three-cycle subgroup, so assume
$r\in G$. Full image supplies an odd element; Lemma\ref{lem:finite} gives a
section inside$G$.

If$G$ is non-full, apply the lemma to \emph{every} odd$g\in G$. Each has the
same transposition at all three local components. Fix one odd$t$. For every
even$a\in G$, both$at$ and$t$ have common odd components, so$a=(at)t^{-1}$
has a common even component. Hence$G\le L$. Its kernel is a subgroup of the
diagonal$A_3$, so has order 1 or 3. Full image gives$|G|=6$or 18; the first case
is$H_h$ and the second$L$.

Distinct cell-orbit payoffs realize$H_h$. For$L$, give all players the same
utility: zero on repeated labels and otherwise the sign of the permutation
of$(0,1,2)$. If local maps$f_j,f_k$ differ at$a$, put$a$ in both coordinates
and choose the third action so its image completes all three labels. The
source value is zero and the target nonzero, a contradiction. Thus all local
maps equal$f$, and orientation preservation is exactly
$\operatorname{sign}(p)\operatorname{sign}(f)=1$. This proves the complete
group is$L$. Both groups contain bare$A_3$ and a section$H_h$. Neither can
contain a matching section: an odd player transposition fixes a player, but
its common odd strategy map is nonidentity there.
\end{proof}
The normalization proof applies separately to exact, cardinal and ordinal
symmetry. Cardinal means positive affine utility transport; ordinal means
complete pure-profile order transport, including ties. Playerwise min--max
normalization and distinct-level ranks identify those groups literally with
exact automorphism groups on the same domains; constants are normalized to
zero. These facts are proved in the companion utility-invariance record.
No mixed-equilibrium invariance under arbitrary ordinal maps is assumed.
