\title{Symmetry and Player Interchangeability in Finite Games}
\author{Nicholas Ham}

\begin{abstract}
A permutation formula can move payoff recipients and strategy coordinates
in incompatible directions. For at least three players, the resulting
condition is equivalent to full symmetry together with identical payoff
functions, a restriction absent in two-player games. We give a self-contained
account of this established correction and distinguish it from questions of
player interchangeability and assignment indifference. For typed game
automorphisms we separate full player image, coherent lifts, and full symmetry
under a matching. We classify the non-full standard three-player ternary
actions and prove that six is the only player count admitting a standard
ternary game with full player image and a nonsplit player projection. An
explicit six-player common-interest game attains the boundary. Restricted
fairness consequences clarify the role of common utility calibration and
invariant recommendations without imposing a general fairness definition.
The indexing theorem, group constructions and multiplier inputs retain their
established attributions; the structural classifications are the extensions
considered here.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} finite games; symmetry conventions; player interchangeability; group extensions.
