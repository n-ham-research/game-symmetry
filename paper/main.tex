\begin{abstract}
A finite game's automorphisms may realize every player permutation without
admitting a coherent choice of such realizations. We distinguish surjectivity
of the actual player projection, existence of a section, and existence of a
strategy-trivial section. For three-player games with three strategies, we
classify the non-full standard full-image actions: their complete groups have
orders six or eighteen, and both projections split. A local-affine reduction
proves splitting for standard ternary games whenever the player count is not
divisible by three. An explicit six-player common-interest game with binary
payoffs has exactly eighteen winning profiles, complete automorphism group of
order 2160, and a nonsplit player projection with kernel of order three. Combining the
local-affine reduction with the classical alternating-group multiplier
theorem shows that six is the only possible player count for standard
ternary nonsplitting. For at least five players other than six, full actual
player image alone forces splitting. Group constructions
and covering-group principles used here are established prior mathematics;
novelty of the game classifications and boundary applications remains under
investigation. Essential small finite certificates are specified separately
from the general proofs.
\end{abstract}
