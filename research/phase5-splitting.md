# Phase 5: four-player ternary splitting

Status: **candidate extension**. The algebraic reduction and the bounded finite
certificate below establish the proposed four-player result, subject to the
independent checks recorded in the Phase 5 review. Novelty remains unverified.
The later P5-S4–S6 give separate arbitrary-player sufficient conditions,
and P5-S7 supplies a six-player obstruction. In particular P5-S6 gives an
alternate entirely algebraic proof of P5-S1; the regular-cycle certificate
remains an independent finite verification, not a necessary proof dependency.

We use `(g s)_{p(i)}=f_i(s_i)` for `g=(p;(f_i))`; composition is right to left.
Thus `(P_p s)_j=s_{p^{-1}(j)}`. A standard subgroup is player-transitive and
strategy-trivial: an element fixing a player has identity component there.
Full symmetry means containment of a strategy-trivial copy of the entire
player group, after any allowed local relabeling.

## Statement

**Candidate P5-S1.** Let a four-player game have exactly three pure strategies
per player. If its actual exact automorphism group is standard symmetric and
projects onto `S4`, then that projection has a section.

This proves coherent interchangeability, not full matching symmetry. The
four-player ternary opposite-face game in `phase5-mechanisms.md` has a split
nonfull group of order 72. Together with Phase 4, P5-S1 excludes standard,
full-image nonsplitting games on three strategies at three or four players.
P5-S6 below also rules them out at five players; P5-S7 exhibits the first
possible case, at six players.

**Candidate P5-S2 (regular-cycle group lemma).** Every subgroup of
`S3 wr S4` containing a strategy-trivial regular cyclic subgroup of order
four and projecting onto `S4` has a section. This is an abstract embedded-group
statement; payoff realizability is not needed. Its proof is a finite
certificate involving 251 representatives and 21 distinct generated groups
among those representatives, rather than enumeration of all subgroups.

**Candidate P5-S3 (large local-sign kernel).** Let `G <= S3 wr S4` project
onto `S4`, and let `B` be its local-sign quotient in `C2 wr S4`. If the
pointwise-player kernel of `B` contains the even-sign subgroup `E <= C2^4`,
then either `G` contains all independent local three-cycles, or it is locally
conjugate into the subgroup whose local maps fix a designated strategy in
each fibre. In the latter case the conjugated group is exactly `B` acting by
local sign changes on `{0,1,-1}`. This algebraic dichotomy needs no standardness.

The proof of P5-S1 uses P5-S2 and the special determinant-even case of P5-S3.

## 1. A small quotient detects regular four-cycles

Identify each local strategy set with `F3`. Every permutation of this set has
unique form `x -> a x+b`, with `a in {1,-1}` and `b in F3`. Let

`R=F3^4`, `W=R semidirect (C2^4 semidirect S4)`,

and let `rho:W -> C2 wr S4` forget local translations. For the group `G` put
`B=rho(G)` and `K=B intersect C2^4`.

If an element of `B` above a player four-cycle has order four, any lift to
`G` has order four or twelve: the fourth power has trivial local signs and
player permutation, so is a translation of order dividing three. In the
order-twelve case its cube has order four and still projects to a four-cycle.
Every cyclic subgroup generated by such an order-four lift is automatically
strategy-trivial: the nonidentity powers of a regular four-cycle fix no
player. Its coherent matching then relabels the generator to the bare
four-cycle, by the audited matching theorem (Ham 4.23).

It remains to analyze the case in which **every** four-cycle lift in `B`
has order eight. For a binary signed permutation `(b,p)`, with `b in F2^4`,
set `d(b,p)=sum_i b_i`. This is a homomorphism. Above a four-cycle,

`(b,p)^4=d(b,p) (1,1,1,1)`.

Thus the remaining case has `d=1` on every four-cycle lift. In particular
`K` contains no odd-weight vector: multiplying a lift by such a kernel vector
would yield `d=0`.

Conjugation makes `K` an `S4`-invariant subspace of `F2^4`. The elementary
module argument gives only `0,T,E,V`, where `T` is the constant line, `E`
the even-weight space, and `V=F2^4`: a nonconstant kernel vector, added to
its image under a transposition of unequal entries, gives `e_i+e_j`; its
conjugates span `E`. The preceding paragraph excludes `V`.

If `K=0`, the group `B` itself is a copy of `S4` and its four-cycle lifts
have order four, a contradiction.

If `K=T`, every transposition lift is an involution: its square has support
in the two swapped coordinates and also lies in `T`, so must be zero.
Disjoint transposition lifts have commutator either zero or the central
constant vector `z`. This alternative is the same for all disjoint pairs,
by conjugacy and independence from the chosen lifts. If it is zero, choose
adjacent lifts. Adjusting them by `z` successively along the adjacent chain
kills all adjacent braid defects without changing involutions or distant
commutations. The Coxeter presentation then gives a section, contradicting
order eight for every four-cycle lift.

If the distant commutator is `z`, every lift of a double transposition
squares to `z`: write it as a product of lifts of its disjoint factors and
use their anticommutation. But `B` is standard. Indeed the image under `rho`
of a standard subgroup of `G` is again standard and has the same player
image: strategy triviality ensures its projection is injective. Every
transitive subgroup of `S4` contains a double transposition. To see this,
its order is divisible by four; a Sylow 2-subgroup either has a four-cycle,
whose square is a double transposition, or has two distinct commuting
involutions, whose possibilities in `S4` include a double transposition
among them or their product. The standard lift of this involution must be
an involution, contradicting its square `z`.

Only `K=E` remains. Since `V/E=C2` is a trivial player module, `B` is the
inverse image of the graph of a homomorphism `S4 -> C2`. That homomorphism
is either trivial or player parity. The four-cycle condition chooses parity.
Consequently

`B=D := {(b,p): sum_i b_i = parity(p)}`.

This is a quotient-group reduction, not an assumption that an abstract
nonsplit group is realizable as the full group of a game.

## 2. The determinant-even quotient cannot be an actual game group

We first prove P5-S3. Put `N=G intersect R`. It is a linear subspace of
`F3^4`, invariant under the signed permutation action of `B`. In particular
it is invariant under the diagonal even-sign group `E`, of order eight.
Its four coordinate characters are pairwise distinct. Averaging over `E`
with a coordinate character projects any vector onto that coordinate:

`v_i e_i = (1/8) sum_{e in E} chi_i(e) e v`.

All arithmetic here is in `F3`, where eight is invertible. A nonzero `N`
therefore contains a coordinate line; transitivity on players gives all
four lines. Thus `N=0` or `N=R`.

If `N=0`, projection identifies `G` with the graph of a cocycle
`c:B -> R`, satisfying `c(gh)=c(g)+g c(h)`. Average its restriction over
`E` to find `w` with `c(e)=w-e w` for every `e in E`. Conjugating by the
translation `-w` makes `c|E=0`. For arbitrary `g in B,e in E`, normality
then gives

`c(g)=c(ge)=c((g e g^-1)g)=(g e g^-1)c(g)`.

The only vector fixed by all even sign changes is zero. Hence `c(g)=0`
for all `g`, and the conjugated `G` is exactly the local-sign group `B`.
This proves P5-S3. Importantly, it would be invalid to average over the
whole group `B`: in the determinant-even case its order 192 is divisible
by three. Only the normal subgroup of order eight is averaged over.

Apply this to `B=D` from section 1. If `N=R`, invariance under independent
translations makes each player's utility constant in every strategy
coordinate. Player transitivity equates these constants, so the game's
actual group is all of `W`, whose sign quotient is not `D`. This contradicts
`B=D`.

If `N=0`, conjugate as above so `G=D` with local maps `x -> +/-x` on
`{0,1,-1}`. We show that invariance under `D` already forces ordinary full
symmetry, again contradicting that `G` is the actual group.

On any profile containing a zero coordinate, every arbitrary local sign
change has the same effect as an even local sign change: add a sign change
at a zero coordinate to correct parity without changing the profile.
Thus every utility is invariant under arbitrary sign changes there.

On profiles without zero coordinates, the kernel `E` is transitive on each
of the two product-sign classes. Fix a focal player `i`. A player
transposition fixing `i` has a lift in `D` with an odd number of local sign
changes. That lift changes the product sign. Exact invariance equates the
two possible utility values for player `i`. Thus that player's utility is
also invariant under arbitrary local sign changes on this part of the
profile space. This applies separately to each player.

All independent local sign changes consequently belong to the actual
automorphism group. Combining them with the given lifts of all player
permutations supplies all bare player permutations. In fact this forces a
strict overgroup of `D`. Therefore the determinant-even possibility cannot
occur as the full exact group of a game. Every game in P5-S1 has a regular
order-four lift, reducing its splitting to P5-S2.

## 3. Complete certificate for the regular-cycle case

After matching relabeling assume the bare cycle
`r=(0 1 2 3)` belongs to `G`. Choose any lift of a player three-cycle.
Its image together with `r` generates `S4`. Conjugating by a power of `r`
and, if necessary, taking the inverse of the lift normalizes its player
image to `a=(0 2 1)` fixing player 3. Indeed the eight three-cycles are two
orbits under conjugation by `<r>`, interchanged by inversion.

There are exactly `6^4=1296` lifts of this one player three-cycle in `W`.
Conjugation by a common local strategy permutation preserves `r`; taking
one representative per such orbit leaves exactly 251 lifts. This count can
also be obtained from Burnside's lemma:

`(6^4 + 3*2^4 + 2*3^4)/6 = 251`.

For each representative `g`, form `H=<r,g>` by exact breadth-first closure.
Among the chosen representatives there are 21 distinct generated embedded
groups. For each the certificate supplies elements `r',a' in H` with
player images `r,a` and with `<r',a'>` of order exactly 24. The latter
subgroup projects onto `S4` and therefore maps isomorphically onto it. Its
inverse projection is the desired section. Hence every `H`, and every
overgroup `G` in the statement, has a section.

The search uses `r'^4=a'^3=(r'a')^2=1` to find the pairs, but the verifier
additionally generates the pair subgroup and checks its order is exactly
24. Thus no unverified use of a triangle-group presentation is needed.
The section need not contain the original standard generator `r`.

### Precisely specified verifier

`experiments/phase5_splitting.py` uses lexicographic permutations of four
players and three strategies. The identity is first in both lists. A typed
element is the player permutation followed by four local strategy
permutations; its integer encoding is
`player_index*6^4 + local_tuple_index`, where the tuple index uses base six
with the first local component most significant. Its multiplication is

`(p;f)(q;k) = (p q; (f_{q(i)} k_i)_i)`.

The verifier must perform all of the following:

1. Generate all 1296 local tuples, take the lexicographically least tuple
   under the six simultaneous conjugations, and retain one lift per key.
   Assert there are 251 keys.
2. For every retained lift, close `<r,g>` starting from the identity by left
   multiplication by both generators. Finite positive-word closure is the
   subgroup because inverses occur as powers.
3. For every new group search all elements above `r` with fourth power one
   and all elements above `a` with cube one. Retain a pair for which the
   product is an involution. Independently close its generated subgroup and
   check that it contains exactly 24 elements.
4. Check each pair belongs to its group, and that its player images are
   exactly `r,a`. Assert no group lacks a certificate, and compare the
   complete saved generator/order/pair records with the recomputed JSON.

Run `python3 experiments/phase5_splitting.py --verify` to reproduce the
complete finite part. It requires only the Python standard library, no
GAP/Sage or cached source downloads. Neither GAP nor Sage is installed in
the checked environment; that fact motivated the transparent small verifier
rather than an undocumented external group-system call.

## Utility-level corollary and remaining boundary

Canonical min-max and level-rank normalization identify respectively the
cardinal and ordinal automorphism groups with the exact automorphism groups
of games on the same strategy domains, as proved in
`utility-invariance.md`. Applying P5-S1 to those normalized games gives the
same four-player splitting conclusion separately for cardinal-standard/full-
cardinal-image and ordinal-standard/full-ordinal-image games. This uses no
expected-utility preservation under ordinal transformations.

The proof is specifically four-player. Its regular-cycle certificate depends
on `S4=<r,a>`, and its binary quotient analysis uses the exceptional
four-player central-kernel analysis. The arbitrary-player results below are proved separately;
no extrapolation from this finite certificate is made.

## Arbitrary-player sufficient conditions

The same quotient method gives useful unbounded statements even though it
does not settle arbitrary-player ternary splitting. For this section let
`n>=3`, `R=F3^n`, `B=rho(G) <= C2 wr S_n`, and
`K=B intersect C2^n`. Write `T=<(-1,...,-1)>` for the constant sign line and
`E` for the even-sign group. The distinction `K=0,T,E,V` follows from the
same permutation-module argument over `F2` used above.

**Candidate P5-S4 (large sign kernel forces full matching).** If `G` is the
actual exact automorphism group of a ternary game, its player image is `S_n`,
and `K` contains `E`, then the game is fully symmetric under some matching.
Standardness is unnecessary.

**Proof.** The `n` coordinate characters of `E` are distinct and nontrivial
for every `n>=3`; the averaging denominators are powers of two, hence
invertible in `F3`. The proof of P5-S3 therefore applies with four replaced
by `n`: either `N=G intersect R` is `R`, or `N=0` and local translation
conjugation makes `G=B`. In the first case invariance makes every utility
constant, and full player image equates the constants, giving the full
ambient group and full matching symmetry.

In the second case `K` is `E` or `V`. If `K=V`, the group contains all bare
player permutations after cancelling local signs. If `K=E`, the quotient
by `E` is the graph of one of the two homomorphisms `S_n -> C2`, trivial or
parity. The trivial graph already contains all bare player permutations.
For the parity graph, the zero-coordinate absorption argument above still
applies. On the all-nonzero profiles the kernel has precisely two product-
sign orbits, which a lifted transposition fixing the focal player exchanges.
Such a transposition exists for every focal player when `n>=3`. Therefore
all individual local sign changes preserve the exact utilities, again
supplying all bare player permutations. Undoing the local translation
conjugation supplies a full matching in the original labels. ∎

**Candidate P5-S5 (constant sign kernel removes translation obstructions).**
Suppose `G <= S3 wr S_n` projects onto `S_n`, with `K=T`. Then `G` is
conjugate by local translations to `N semidirect B`, where
`N=G intersect F3^n`. In particular, a section of `B -> S_n` gives a section
of `G -> S_n`. Consequently every standard full-image ternary group with
constant sign kernel splits, for all `n>=3`. This last assertion is an
abstract embedded-group result and does not need payoff realizability.

**Proof.** The subspace `N` is invariant under `B`. In the quotient
`(R/N) semidirect B`, the image of `G` is a cocycle graph, as in section 2.
The central normal subgroup `T` of `B` has order two and acts as minus the
identity on `R/N`. Averaging the cocycle over `T` and translating makes it
zero there. The same normality calculation then makes each cocycle value
`T`-fixed. Since `R/N` has characteristic three, its only vector equal to
its negative is zero. Hence the cocycle vanishes. Lift the conjugating
translation from `R/N` to `R`. The resulting group has all translations in
`N` and, above each `b in B`, an element with translation in `N`; cancelling
that translation supplies `(0,b)`. Thus it is exactly `N semidirect B`.

For completeness the needed binary splitting statement follows directly
from the central-kernel Coxeter argument. Transposition lifts in `B` are
involutions. At `n>=5` disjoint-lift commutators have support on four
coordinates and belong to the constant line, so vanish; braid defects are
removed successively by the central constant flip. At `n=3` there are no
disjoint relations, and the same braid adjustment works. At `n=4` the sole
extra possibility has every double-transposition lift of order four; as
proved above it cannot admit a player-transitive strategy-trivial subgroup.
Standardness descends from `G` to `B`, so excludes that possibility. This
constructs the claimed section in every stated case. ∎

These results isolate the remaining possible obstruction sharply. A
standard full-image ternary game at `n>=5` whose projection does not split
must have **zero local-sign kernel**. Its nontrivial pointwise-player kernel,
if any, is entirely a subspace of `F3^n`; its sign quotient is a copy of
`S_n`. This is a representation-theoretic restriction, not an existence
claim or a complete arbitrary-player classification.

**Candidate P5-S6 (player counts not divisible by three).** Every standard
full-image ternary game with `n>=3` players and `3` not dividing `n` has a
section of its player projection. Thus in particular the five-player ternary
splitting question has an affirmative answer. Together with P4-T1, the first
possible player count is six, attained by P5-S7 below.

**Proof.** By P5-S4 and P5-S5 it suffices to consider zero local-sign kernel.
Then `B` maps isomorphically onto `S_n`. A point stabilizer acts on its local
sign fibre by a homomorphism `S_(n-1) -> C2`, either trivial or parity. The
coherent-transport argument in `phase4-binary-verification.md`, Attack 2,
therefore gives local sign labels in which `B` is the ordinary permutation
complement or the common-parity sign complement. Their actions on `R=F3^n`
are respectively permutation of coordinates and that representation twisted
by player parity. Twisting by a scalar changes no invariant subspaces.

Every permutation-invariant subspace `N` of `F3^n` is one of
`0,T_3,E_3,R`, where `T_3` is the constant line and `E_3` consists of vectors
whose coordinate sum is zero. Indeed a nonconstant vector in `N`, subtracted
from its image under a transposition of unequal entries, supplies a nonzero
multiple of `e_i-e_j`. Conjugates span `E_3`. Thus `N` is `E_3` or `R`
if it contains a nonconstant vector, and otherwise is `0` or `T_3`.

Since `3` does not divide `n`,
`R=T_3 direct-sum E_3`, with the equivariant projection onto the constant
line given by `v -> n^-1(sum_i v_i)(1,...,1)`. Hence each possible `N` has
a `B`-invariant complement `M` in `R`, under either the ordinary or
parity-twisted action.

The image of `G` in `(R/N) semidirect B` is the graph of a cocycle
`c:B -> R/N`. The equivariant inverse `R/N -> M` lifts it to a cocycle
`c_tilde:B -> R`. Every element `(c_tilde(b),b)` lies in `G`, because its
translation has the required residue modulo `N`. These elements form a
subgroup projecting isomorphically onto `B` and thence onto `S_n`. They are
the required section. The proof does not assume that every cocycle is a
coboundary; lifting the cocycle through an invariant direct summand suffices.
∎

When `3` divides `n`, the constant line lies inside the sum-zero subspace.
The equivariant direct-sum step fails, leaving a possible modular extension
obstruction. This identifies a precise algebraic reason to test six players;
it does not by itself prove an obstruction exists or is payoff-realizable.

For `n>=5` with `3` not dividing `n`, P5-S6 in fact needs only full actual
player image: the constant-sign-kernel binary group splits without the
standardness hypothesis at these player counts, while the other branches
already require none. Standardness is retained in P5-S6 to include `n=4`.

**Candidate P5-S7 (the six-player obstruction is attained).** There is a
six-player, three-strategy, common-interest game with payoffs in `{0,1}`
whose full exact automorphism group is standard, has full player image,
and has no player-projection section. Its group has order 2160 and
pointwise-player kernel `C3`. Consequently six is the least player count
for a ternary game with those three properties. The same minimality holds
separately for exact, cardinal and ordinal symmetry.

The explicit code, full-group proof and independent finite certificates are
in `phase5-six-player-obstruction.md`. The group and its prior monomial/
permutation realization are not asserted new; the cited ATLAS data and
Hadamard-matrix source supply those prior structures. The mathematical game
application and minimal boundary remain candidate contributions with priority
unresolved.

**Candidate P5-S8 (necessary type of every larger ternary obstruction).**
Let `n>=5`, and suppose an actual ternary game is standard, has player image
`S_n`, and has nonsplitting player projection. After local relabeling its
local-sign quotient is the common-parity copy of `S_n`, its complete
pointwise-player kernel consists of the three common translations, and
its group has order `3 n!`. Odd player permutations invert that kernel;
even ones centralize it. Necessarily `3` divides `n`.

This is a necessary represented-extension type, not a theorem that this
extension exists at every multiple of three or that its payoffs must be
isomorphic to the six-player code game.

**Proof.** P5-S4 and P5-S5 exclude all nonzero local-sign kernels. Hence
`B` is a complement copy of `S_n`, and its action on `R=F3^n` is ordinary
or common-parity-twisted permutation, as proved in P5-S6. Its invariant
translation kernel `N` is `0,T_3,E_3,R`. The cases `N=0` and `N=R` give,
respectively, an immediate section and a constant-payoff game with full
ambient group. We exclude `N=E_3` and the ordinary-action case `N=T_3`.

If `N=E_3`, the quotient `R/N` is one-dimensional, with trivial or parity
action. The corresponding cocycle restricted to `A_n` is a homomorphism
into the additive group `F3`, because `A_n` acts trivially on the quotient.
For `n>=5`, `A_n` is perfect: it is generated by three-cycles, and each
three-cycle or its inverse is the commutator of `(a b)(d e)` and
`(b c)(d e)` for five distinct labels. The restriction is therefore zero.
The cocycle descends to `S_n/A_n=C2`. With trivial coefficient action the
involution relation says twice its value is zero, so the value is zero.
With sign action its arbitrary value is a coboundary `w-(-w)=2w`, since
two is invertible in `F3`. Thus a translation makes the quotient cocycle
zero and reconstructs `G=E_3 semidirect B`, providing a section. This
contradicts nonsplitting, so `N=T_3`.

Suppose now the action is ordinary. Common translations are then central.
Any lift `(v,p)` of a player transposition satisfies `(v,p)^2=c z`, with
`z=(1,...,1)`. At every fixed coordinate this says `2 v_i=c`; shifting the
lift by the common translation `-(c/2)z` makes it an involution. Its
translation entries at all fixed coordinates are then zero. Choose such
lifts for the adjacent transpositions. A commutator of disjoint lifts has
translation support on their four moved coordinates and belongs to `T_3`;
since `n>=5`, it must be zero. The cube of a product of adjacent lifts
also lies in `T_3`, but has zero translation at every coordinate outside
their three moved players, so is zero. These are exactly the Coxeter
relations, yielding a section. Thus the ordinary action is impossible.

Only the common-parity-twisted action with kernel `T_3` remains. This gives
order `3 n!` and the stated inversion/centralization rule directly from
conjugation of translations. Finally P5-S6 forces `3` to divide `n`. ∎

For the remaining player counts `n=9,12,...`, P5-S8 reduces the question to
a sign-twisted cyclic three-extension in its specified local representation,
plus actual payoff realizability and standardness. Classification of abstract
covering groups alone would not remove those last represented-game checks.
A source-supported analysis of the relevant alternating-group extensions
is a natural next step; no unsupported general nonexistence claim is made.
