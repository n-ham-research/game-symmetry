## Covering groups are prior work

**Primary full text inspected:** Cao, Li, Tan and Yang, *On group structures
of strategic-form games*, Fundamental Research4(3),540–549, DOI
[10.1016/j.fmre.2022.06.015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11197539/).
Online publication July2022; issue collection May2024. Definition1 and Lemma1
identify the maximal subgroup preserving each individual orbit of a partition.
Theorem3 characterizes player-group realizability by projecting such covering
motion groups; §5.2 attributes its quotient formulation to Peleg, Rosenmüller
and Sudhölter, Corollary3.20. Our payoff-cell orbit closure is this prior
covering construction in the typed ambient action. Assigning distinct values
to cell orbits is not a new realization principle. The candidate contribution
is its application to the specific ternary group classification and lifting
obstructions, rather than invention of orbit closure. The inspected results
do not classify sections of the actual player projection under standardness
or establish the particular three-strategy boundaries here.

This is a substantive new comparison relative to Phase4. The earlier vague
next-tier reference is now a concrete source attribution in the proof draft.

## Alternating payoff arrays are prior work

**Primary full text inspected:** Hao and Cheng,
[*On Skew-Symmetric Games*, arXiv:1712.02962v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1712.02962v1),
Definition11/equation19, §4.1 Propositions32–33, §4.2 equations48–49 and
Theorem36. Their framework contains the signed opposite-face payoff array:
focal utility alternates in the opponents, with its own-action coefficient
free. At n=m+1 their zero-sum conclusion is explicitly prior; for n>m+1 all
skew-symmetric payoffs vanish. Therefore neither the alternating construction,
its player-parity covariance nor its zero-sum property is claimed as new.
The exact full typed group, failure of every matching, binary coarsening that
retains that group, and the reduction statements are the candidate results
requiring further comparison. This construction alone cannot give a
nonzero fixed-three-strategy skew array beyond four players.

## Earlier symmetry definitions

The author-hosted
[Rosenmüller project summary](https://wwwhomes.uni-bielefeld.de/jr/Elmarkt/symmain.pdf),
pp.1–2, defines a player symmetry as a permutation admitting compatible
strategy renamings. This is full player image, not a coherent section. It
studies symmetry-preserving extensive representations; the summary alone
does not establish any of the present small-strategy splitting assertions.

The institutional [April1996 working-paper record](https://webdoc.sub.gwdg.de/ebook/e/bielefeld/253.html)
links a twenty-page primary PDF of Peleg–Rosenmüller–Sudhölter, *The canonical
extensive form of a game form, PartI: Symmetries*. A public PDF was retrieved
into temporary storage, but its extracted mathematics has broken font
encoding and the web screenshot failed. No new theorem-body verdict is based
on that extraction. The readable Cao–Li–Tan–Yang text gives a precise route
to Corollary3.20; a legible original comparison remains desirable. Do not
confuse the 1996 working-paper date with the 1999 published chapter.

## The six-player triple cover is an established group construction

**Primary proof inspected:** Gillespie, Ó Catháin and Praeger,
[*Construction of the outer automorphism of S6 via a complex Hadamard matrix*](https://api.research-repository.uwa.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36067400/Gillespie_et_al_2018_Construction_of_the_Outer.pdf),
2018, Proposition2 and its proof, pp.3–4 of the author version, and the
18-point permutation description on p.5. The paper explicitly establishes
the nonsplit group3.S6, its subgroup3.A6, and its Hadamard/monomial realization.
The [ATLAS A6 representation page](https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/alt/A6/)
independently supplies generators for the degree18 representation used here.
Neither the group, its nonsplitting nor the existence of this representation
is a new contribution. The candidate application is an exact binary
common-interest game whose **full typed** group is that representation,
with an explicit strategy-trivial transitive subgroup, together with the
lower bound excluding ternary nonsplitting at fewer than six players.
The finite verifier reconstructs these game properties from literal generator
data and the eighteen winning profiles; it does not infer them merely from
the ATLAS group name. Priority of the game application remains unresolved.
