# Proposed paper on symmetry and lifting

Nicholas Ham. Research proposal, 16 October 2026. Candidate contributions
remain subject to source comparison and author/external mathematical review.

## Central question and proposed scope

Study when the actual player projection of a finite game's automorphism group
has a coherent section, and when a section can be strategy trivial. This
separates full player image, coherent lifts and full matching symmetry. The
main small-strategy narrative now goes beyond the three-player classification:
three strategies permit nonsplitting at six players, but not at fewer players
under standardness and full image. The obstruction is represented by a known
triple-cover group; its game realization and minimality are the contribution
to assess, not invention of that group.

A working title is **Symmetry and lifting in finite games**. The binary
exact-potential strengthening is better developed separately: its main new
step, if novel, is rigidity of the actual typed group, while ordinary binary
potentialness is established prior work. Utility normalizations can be a short
corollary here. Fairness and calibrated assignment should remain a separate
programme.

## Suggested contents

1. **Definitions and prior results.** Exact typed automorphisms, the actual
   player map, strategy-trivial subgroups and matchings. Attribute the group
   framework to Nash/Peleg and subsequent literature and the matching
   distinctions to Ham2021. Give one consistent composition convention.
2. **Binary three-player benchmark.** State Ham's Figure4 and Example5.13 as
   prior work. Supply the short algebraic specialization characterization as
   a refinement for individual payoff assignments, including accidental
   parameter equalities. This motivates rather than claims the old separation.
3. **Complete ternary three-player mechanisms.** State the order6/18 theorem
   and splitting boundary. Give the standard-cycle normalization, normalizer
   reduction and actual-payoff orbit-closure argument. Attribute covering
   groups to the prior literature. Put the32/11-case finite lemma and complete
   verifier in supplementary material, with its essential role explicit.
4. **Splitting beyond three players.** Present the local-affine decomposition
   overF3, the large-sign and constant-sign kernel lemmas, and splitting when
   the number of players is not divisible by3. This supplies a conceptual
   four- and five-player result; the independent251-case four-player
   calculation belongs in the supplement, not the main proof.
5. **Six-player ternary nonsplitting.** Give the eighteen-profile binary
   common-interest game, its exact full group, its transitive strategy-trivial
   subgroup and the perfect-even-preimage obstruction. Credit ATLAS and the
   Hadamard/triple-cover literature. State the minimal-player conclusion with
   the standardness and equal ternary strategy hypotheses beside it.
6. **Other realizations and reductions.** Give the common-interest order18
   witness and four-action binary nonsplit witness. Include the arbitrary-m
   opposite-face binary construction if it supports the narrative rather
   than crowding the paper; credit signed arrays to Hao–Cheng. Explain why
   different extensions can reduce to one another and why common interest
   obstructs reduction to Example5.13. State the clone quotient theorem with
   multiplicities and separate player deletion.
7. **Open problems.** Classification at four-player ternary size; complete
   description of nonsplit mechanisms at multiples of3; symbolic replacement
   of the remaining three-player finite lemma; reductions allowing player
   deletion or utility changes. Make no universal unique-core conjecture
   without an explicit reduction relation.
