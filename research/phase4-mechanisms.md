# Phase 4: Figure 4, mechanisms and reductions

Status: Ham's Figure 4 and Example 5.13 are **previously established by Ham**.
The algebraic individual-game deduction and reduction comparisons below are
**candidate extensions/reformulations**, with no novelty claim. Internal review
is not external peer review. All games are finite, with nonempty strategy sets
and exact real utilities. Compose right to left, with
(P_p s)_j=s_{p^{-1}(j)}. Strategies and players remain typed.

## What completeness means

The exhaustive seven-family check in `classification-audit.md` verifies Figure 4
for equality families generated by player-transitive typed subgroups, modulo
conjugacy of utility-cell partitions. This is an audit of Ham's prior work,
not discovery of seven new families. The completeness proof has three steps:
every symmetric game's actual automorphism group is one of the enumerated
subgroups; an invariant utility function is constant on its utility-cell orbits;
and every such function is an assignment of the orbit parameters. Quotienting
those partitions and testing refinement gives the seven nodes and their exact
covers. Distinct parameter-block values provide the witness for the converse
of every claimed family inclusion, so the check covers all real assignments,
not merely numerical samples. This does not identify a family with a single
concrete game or a single automorphism type.

## An algebraic proof of individual-game uniqueness in the binary case

**P4-M1 (candidate reformulation).** Every three-player, two-strategy game whose
actual player image is S_3 and which is not fully symmetric up to isomorphism
is isomorphic to a specialization of Ham's Example 5.13 with beta != gamma.
In particular it is standard; standardness need not be separately assumed.

Here is a short deduction independent of enumerating the seven families. Write
V=F_2^3, z=(1,1,1), E={v:sum v_i=0}, and let K be the player kernel. Conjugation
makes K an S_3-invariant vector subspace. A nonconstant vector and its image
under a transposition sum to a coordinate pair; conjugation generates E.
Thus K is 0, <z>, E, or V.

For K=V the game is constant and fully symmetric. For K=E each focal utility
depends only on profile parity. A transposition has a fixed third player, so
its lift cannot change parity unless that third player's utility is constant.
Full player image transports constancy to all players. In the nonconstant
case each transposition lift preserves parity and equates the swapped focal
utilities. In both cases ordinary full symmetry follows.

For K=<z>, every transposition lift is involutory: its square has flip support
on just the two moved coordinates but lies in <z>, hence is zero. For lifts
t,u of adjacent transpositions, (tu)^3 is 1 or z. Multiplying t by z toggles
this defect. The resulting involutions satisfy the S_3 presentation and give
a complement. For K=0 the entire group already is a complement.

In any complement, a player's stabilizer C_2 acts on its two strategies either
trivially or by their interchange, with the same choice for every player by
conjugacy. In the trivial case the matching theorem makes the complement the
ordinary S_3 action by local labels. In the nontrivial case twist each odd
permutation by z; the stabilizers become trivial, so the same theorem gives,
after local labels, the signed action s -> P_p s + sign(p)z. If K=<z>, z itself
is an automorphism, and the signed lifts therefore also give the ordinary
ones. Consequently a non-full game must have K=0 and signed S_3 action.

Its utility cells have exactly four orbits: homogeneous profiles; minority
players in nonhomogeneous profiles; and the two cyclically distinguished
majority-player cells. Calling the four values alpha, delta, beta, gamma
respectively gives exactly Example 5.13's displayed table (exchange beta and
gamma if the cyclic convention is reversed). If beta=gamma, the table is
ordinary fully symmetric. If beta!=gamma, a nonhomogeneous profile never has
all three focal utilities equal, so every matching supporting full symmetry
would have to use only 000 and 111. That is the ordinary matching, whose
opponent transposition exchanges beta and gamma and fails. This proves both
directions, including all parameter coincidences involving alpha or delta.

The result concerns all individual exact-payoff assignments, not only generic
orbit families. It sharpens the quantifier explanation rather than replacing
Ham's prior classification. `phase3-equilibria.md` supplies the longer general
kernel argument. Its n>=4 standard/full-image theorem further says that this
is the only binary-strategy player count permitting the separation.

## Two genuinely different action obstructions

Keep the actual map q:A->S_n, not just the abstract isomorphism type of A.
A **projection-aware action equivalence** is a player bijection, local strategy
bijections and a group isomorphism intertwining q and every local action.
It preserves existence of a section and of a strategy-trivial section.
It ignores utility magnitudes while retaining the represented full group.
This is a precise strong notion of the same symmetry mechanism, not a claim
that it is the unique useful definition.

Example 5.13 with beta!=gamma has A=S_3 and K=1. Its projection splits, but
its unique section has nontrivial fixed-player stabilizer action on the two
strategies. The binary-payoff four-strategy construction P3-S1b has
A={(p,T_k): k mod 2=sign(p)}, K=<T_2>, and every transposition lift has order
four. It has no section at all, hence no strategy-trivial section. It is
therefore not fully symmetric under any matching. These cannot be
projection-aware action equivalent: orders, kernels and splitting all differ.
Thus structural uniqueness fails if 'same obstruction' means this natural
represented-extension equivalence. No exhaustive classification of nonbinary
obstructions follows.

## Reductions: the choice changes the answer

A **full-payoff clone quotient** consists of surjections r_i:A_i->B_i and an
exact payoff factorization u_i(s)=v_i(r_1(s_1),...,r_n(s_n)) for every focal i.
Two strategies in one fibre must give the same *whole payoff vector* in every
opponent context. This excludes deleting actions merely because they give
the same own utility or are never equilibria. Both Example 5.13 with
beta!=gamma and P3-S1b have no nontrivial clone quotient. For 5.13 replace one
coordinate of a homogeneous profile: the resulting payoff vector contains
the unequal beta and gamma, so differs from the original constant vector.
For S1b the simultaneous zero-payoff profiles are exactly the diagonal;
replacing one action there makes the payoff vector nonzero. Thus every pair
of distinct actions is separated in both games.

An **inherited-full-image restriction** selects nonempty B_i subset A_i and
requires some H<=Aut(game) with q(H)=S_n to map these subsets onto each other.
One then restricts the utility table. Together with clone quotients and exact
isomorphisms these form a conservative reduction system preserving a specified
source of the symmetry, rather than allowing newly appearing automorphisms.
Both witnesses are irreducible under these reductions. In 5.13, H must be the
whole S_3, whose player stabilizer interchanges that player's two actions.
In S1b, H must be the whole order-12 group: a proper full-image H would have
order six, hence would be a section, already impossible. Its player stabilizer
is C_4 acting regularly on that player's four actions via T_1, so has no proper
nonempty invariant subset. Thus no proper inherited-full-image rectangular
restriction exists. Clone freeness handles quotients; isomorphisms preserve
these statements. This proves irreducibility only for the stated reduction
system, not for all mathematically natural systems.

Crucially, a weaker, equally natural notion has a different outcome. An
**emergent-symmetry restriction** permits arbitrary nonempty rectangular
subsets, then computes the restricted game's full automorphism group afresh.
Restrict S1b to B_0=B_1=B_2={0,1}. Direct substitution in its defining formula
gives exactly Example 5.13 with

    alpha=0, beta=1, gamma=0, delta=0.

Thus S1b *does reduce to Example 5.13* under unrestricted rectangular
restriction, despite the distinct extension obstruction of the original game.
Its signed transposition automorphisms are newly emergent: the original group
has no full-image subgroup preserving these subsets. In particular S1b alone
does not refute the user's minimal-reduction conjecture when this weaker
reduction is allowed. Splitting need not be monotone under restrictions with
new automorphisms.

`experiments/phase4_reductions.py` checks all 6^3=216 choices of two actions per
player, retaining all utilities exactly. Four restrictions are standard,
full-player-image and non-full: precisely the common adjacent pairs {0,1},
{0,3}, {1,2}, {2,3}. Two are standard but lack full player image, and the
remaining 210 lack both properties. All six common pairs are standard (their
bare C_3 rotations survive). The exact {0,1} identity above is tested against
the independently extracted pinned source table, not merely its group type.
This finite census is not a theorem about arbitrary larger games.

An arbitrary utility transformation, player deletion or strategically
irrelevant extension requires its own definition and preservation theorem.
No assertion here rules out a common obstruction after a more permissive
reduction. Conversely, defining reduction so narrowly that examples become
irreducible must not be presented as resolving the unrestricted uniqueness
question. The ternary orientation construction below supplies a counterexample
with no Example-5.13 reduction under these more permissive exact operations.
Classifying further mechanisms requires additional hypotheses and remains open.

## Independent S1b proof check

The analytic proof in `phase3-splitting-proof.md` checks out without relying on
the 82,944-element computation. Its six displayed orbits have sizes 4+5*12=64;
only the diagonal has simultaneous zero utilities. That forces all local maps
of any automorphism to be one common permutation f. On profiles with two equal
actions a and different b, the unique positive majority payoff is selected by
a's parity and cyclic orientation, forcing parity(f(a))=parity(a)+sign(p).
For even p there are just four parity-preserving permutations of Z/4: id,
T_2, negation, and 2-minus. The last two fail at profile (0,1,2), whose player-0
utility is one while each reflected profile gives zero. For odd p, divide by
the known lift (p,T_1) and reduce to the even case. Hence the group is exactly
the claimed fibre product. Squaring any odd lift gives T_2, proving no section.
This validates absence of full symmetry as well as nonsplitting. It does not
establish novelty or ternary-strategy minimality.

## A ternary obstruction surviving more permissive reductions

**P4-M2 (candidate extension, no novelty claim).** Set A_i={0,1,2} for three
players and give every player the same utility v(s): zero if any actions
repeat, +1 if s is an even permutation of (0,1,2), and -1 if it is odd. This
standard, full-player-image game is not fully symmetric under any matching.
Its group is

    A={(p;(f,f,f)): p,f in S_3, sign(p)=sign(f)}.

To prove exactness, let arbitrary local maps f_i preserve the utility. If
f_i(a)!=f_j(a), choose the remaining coordinate's action so its image is the
third label. The source has repeated a and utility zero, while the image has
three different labels and utility +/-1, a contradiction. Thus all local maps
are the same f. Applying a common f and player permutation p multiplies the
orientation by sign(f)sign(p), which must equal +1. This proves the displayed
group and its order 18, with kernel A_3. Bare player rotations give standardness;
a common strategy transposition paired with each odd player permutation gives
full player image and an ordinary section S_3->A. Yet a strategy-trivial
full-image subgroup is impossible: every player transposition fixes one focal
player, whose component would have to be identity, whereas its common strategy
map f must be odd. Thus the obstruction is a split extension with unavoidable
nontrivial focal stabilizer action, now with a nontrivial player kernel. It is
projection-aware inequivalent both to Example 5.13 and to S1b.

More significantly, exact rectangular restrictions and full-payoff-factor
quotients preserve the identity u_0=u_1=u_2. Exact player/strategy isomorphisms
also preserve it. Example 5.13 with beta!=gamma violates it, as its majority
players receive these unequal values. Therefore no finite sequence of these
reductions takes this game to a non-full specialization of Example 5.13.
This refutes the proposed universal minimal-reduction conjecture for this
explicit, fairly permissive exact reduction system. It does not address
reductions permitting player-specific utility changes, player deletion or
adding/removing strategically irrelevant payoff terms.

There are no full-payoff clones: for distinct a,b, choose the other two actions
to complete all three labels with a; replacing a by b introduces a repeat and
changes every payoff from +/-1 to zero. No common-interest binary game with
full player image can be non-full, by P4-M1. Hence every two-action-per-player
restriction of this game either loses full player image or becomes fully
symmetric. This is a proof using the binary classification, not a claim that
all restrictions inherit the original group's action.

A bounded payoff-alphabet check gives an additional caution. The orientation
group has four profile orbits: constant profiles, two-equal profiles, even
all-distinct profiles and odd all-distinct profiles. Among all 16 assignments
of values 0 and 1 to these four orbits, twelve have full automorphism group
order 36, two order 108 and two order 1296; all admit a full matching. Thus
three utility values are necessary for a non-full game *within this particular
common-interest invariant family*. No global minimum for arbitrary ternary
witnesses is claimed. The census is exact and tracked in the reduction tests.
