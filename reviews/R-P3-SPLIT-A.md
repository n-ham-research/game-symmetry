## Findings

An affirmative general theorem fails. A three-player, four-strategy game with
payoffs {0,1,2} has exact automorphism group

{(p,common translation k): p∈S_3, k∈Z/4, parity(p)=k mod 2}.

The game is n-transitive and standard via bare C_3, but its extension has no
section because every lift of every transposition has order four. The exact
full group is proved analytically from diagonal profiles, equal-opponent
profiles and an asymmetric difference code. This rules out the concern that
an intended nonsplit subgroup might sit inside a larger split automorphism
group. Complete proof, finite boundary checks and provenance distinction are
in research/phase3-splitting-proof.md.

## Computation actually performed

Ran python3 experiments/phase3_splitting.py. All 82,944 ambient typed maps were
checked against the 64-profile integer game. Exactly 12 automorphisms occur,
with player image 6 and kernel 2; a strategy-trivial transitive order-3
subgroup exists; all six transposition lifts have order 4. Enumerated all eight
subgroups of the exact automorphism group; no order-6 full-image subgroup
occurs. This is independent exact computation supporting, not replacing, the
proof. The expanded game is in phase3-splitting-results.json.

The initial construction used a 16-value injective code for differences. It was
then reduced analytically to three values by preserving a unique difference-one
relation; the final code and proof concern only that smaller game.

## Independent review of P3-E1/E2/E3

Read the complete research/phase3-equilibria.md supplied by C after the
splitting and fairness work. This was an adversarial proof review of the final
argument, not an independent rediscovery. No other agent's review of this
argument was read first. All major steps checked; no mathematical changes
requested.

- Kernel classification: conjugate a nonconstant binary vector by a coordinate
  transposition to obtain every pair sum; this gives exactly 0,T,E,V.
- The E-kernel argument correctly uses a third fixed player to rule out an
  odd total flip in each transposition lift when focal parity utilities are
  nonconstant. All focal parity utilities then agree, giving full symmetry.
- Complement point-stabilizer characters are trivial/sign. The global sign
  twist is an ambient group automorphism, not assumed a payoff symmetry.
  Its compatibility with local relabeling and the matching argument is valid.
- For n>=4 an odd opponent stabilizer transposition fixes a binary utility
  cell, proving equality of A_n and S_n utility-cell orbits.
- For K=T, transposition squares have support two; disjoint commutators have
  support four. These force zero in the claimed degrees. Central adjustment
  of adjacent generators toggles each braid defect by a_i+a_{i+1}, while
  preserving squares and distant commutators. The recursive correction
  supplies the claimed Coxeter section.
- In the exceptional n=4 case, normalize an opponent-transposition lift to
  flip the focal bit. The commutator with the complementary transposition
  makes its flip on the remaining opponent zero. Adjacent normalized lifts
  have product cube with zero focal flip, so that kernel element cannot be z.
  They therefore form an S_3 subgroup whose action on opponent strategy sets
  is strategy trivial. Its matching makes repeated opponent bits available;
  their transposition fixes the opponents and flips the focal bit. This
  proves every own-action payoff difference is zero. Labelings may differ
  between focal players; the conclusion requires no common labeling.
- Every transitive subgroup of S_4 contains a double transposition: a Sylow
  2-subgroup has order at least four, and either contains an order-four
  element (square a double transposition) or an elementary abelian subgroup
  of order four. Thus the order-four lift obstruction excludes standardness
  in precisely the exceptional case.
- The signed n=3 case has the claimed homogeneous/minority cell orbits.
  Majority deviations are signed opponent swaps; minority deviations connect
  to homogeneous profiles. The displayed two-level exact potential works.
- The full-symmetry potential sums binary deviation differences over total
  ones, with the correct sign for each upward deviation. The n=1,2 boundary
  conditions and odd-cycle/matching-pennies counterexamples are correct.
