## Verdict ledger and direct proofs

**4.10: requires clarification; true as an assertion of equipotent sets/torsors, not as a canonical group isomorphism.** A hom-set Iso(X,Y) has no given internal composition when X differs from Y. Choose a:X->Y and b:Y->Z. Then x -> b x a^{-1} is a bijection Iso(X,Y)->Iso(Y,Z), inverse y -> b^{-1} y a. Every nonempty Iso(X,Y) is a left Aut(Y), right Aut(X) bitorsor: for x,y the unique left translator is y x^{-1}, the unique right translator is x^{-1}y. Group structures can be transported after a choice, but this is additional data and not intrinsic. Dependency: 4.9 (typed composition, inverses). No further payoff assertion follows.

**4.11: verified, with coset wording normalized.** Let rho:G->S_N be player projection. It is a homomorphism by the composition definition and ker rho=G_N, the pointwise player stabilizer. rho(g)=rho(h) iff rho(g^{-1}h)=e iff h in gG_N. The quotient consists of these cosets; it is more precise to say 'same coset of G_N in G'. This is pointwise, not setwise stabilizer of N.

**4.12: verified.** gG_N -> rho(g) is well-defined, injective, surjective onto rho(G), and respects products. Depends on 4.11 and projection composition, not strategy triviality.

**4.14: verified.** If rho(g)(i)=rho(h)(i), k=g^{-1}h fixes i. Strategy triviality gives k_i=id, so h_i=g_i k_i=g_i. This concerns the tagged component at i; untagged notation g(a) is ambiguous if a occurs in several A_i.

**4.15: verified.** An element of G_N fixes every player, hence every component is identity by strategy triviality, hence is the identity game bijection. Conversely a trivial player kernel need not force trivial player-stabilizer components; the statement makes no converse. Depends directly on definition (or 4.14 with h=id).

**4.16: verified.** For every reachable ordered pair (i,j), define c_ij=g_i using any g with rho(g)(i)=j. 4.14 proves independence of choice. All g have components c_{i,rho(g)(i)}, and every player permutation in rho(G) has a lift by definition, proving equality of the displayed sets. No maps between different player orbits are asserted. Moreover c_ii=id and c_jk c_ij=c_ik for i,j,k in one orbit: compose representatives and use 4.14. This last coherence identity is implicit and needed in 4.23's original proof.

**4.19: valid as a typed/player-indexed groupoid; false or ill-defined if the displayed set is interpreted literally as untagged functions with their actual set domains.** Let p_i:M->A_i be coordinate projections; matching means each p_i is bijective. Then M_ij=p_j p_i^{-1}. Objects are players i (or tagged sets {i}xA_i), arrows (i,j,M_ij). Identities, inverses and composition follow by cancellation; associativity follows from function composition. This proves the intended statement even with overlapping or equal labels. The indexing is necessary: take n=2, A_1=A_2={0,1,2,3}, M={(a,p(a)):a in A_1} with p=(0123). The literal set of functions is {id,p,p^{-1}}; all functions have domain/codomain the same set, so p composed with p is composable, but p² is absent. Thus the literal untagged collection is not closed. This is an additional source defect, separate from 4.24. No finite enumeration was needed: these are explicit permutations.

**4.20: verified after typed reading; does not require the problematic untagged groupoid assertion.** The i-component of M_phi M_pi is M_{pi(i),phi pi(i)} M_{i,pi(i)}=M_{i,phi pi(i)} by p-coordinate cancellation. Therefore M_phi M_pi=M_{phi pi}. Identity is immediate. Player projection rho(M_pi)=pi, so this homomorphism is injective, even when all strategy sets are singleton. The profile action need not be injective.

**4.21: verified.** 4.20 applied to pi^{-1}pi and pi pi^{-1} gives both inverse identities.

**4.22: verified.** For s in M and j in N, (M_pi s)_j=M_{pi^{-1}(j),j}(s_{pi^{-1}(j)})=s_j. Depends on matching definition/profile action; no utility or transitivity assumption.

**4.23: verified under its actual player-transitivity hypothesis.** Forward: any g=M_pi fixing i has i-component M_ii=id, hence strategy triviality. Reverse: 4.16 and its coherence identity produce c_ij for all pairs because G is transitive. Fix r and put M={(c_ri(a))_i:a in A_r}. Every coordinate map is bijective, so M is a matching. Coherence implies its induced map i->j is c_rj c_ri^{-1}=c_ij. Hence G=M_{rho(G)}. This establishes every omitted existence/coherence step without circular use of 4.24. Cardinalities are automatically equal under transitivity. Dependencies: 4.14/4.16, game-bijection composition, matching definition; 4.20 ensures the constructed image is a subgroup.

General boundary: with intransitive G, strategy triviality alone only supplies one matching on each player orbit. A single global matching exists with G=M_{rho(G)} iff G is strategy trivial AND all A_i have equal cardinality (finite scope). Necessity follows from matching projections. For sufficiency choose orbit base r_O, a common reference set B and arbitrary bijections d_O:B->A_{r_O}; use b->(c_{r_O,i}d_O(b))_i to define M. Then each G-component equals M_{i,rho(g)(i)}. Thus transitivity can be weakened to equal cardinality, but cannot simply be dropped: trivial G on strategy sets of sizes 1 and 2 is strategy trivial and has no matching. This is a candidate generalization, not a correction needed for original 4.23.

**4.24: incorrect as printed.** The repair G<=S_Gamma (full game bijection group), with the existing player n-transitivity, strategy triviality and stabilizer-payoff conditions retained, is sufficient. A full proof and weakest useful equivalent form follow below.

**4.26: verified.** For binary sets let C flip every coordinate. C is a fixed-point-free involution. Every matching has precisely two profiles s,Cs, since in each coordinate the other profile must use the other strategy. Conversely {s,Cs} is a matching. The orbits partition A and count is 2^n/2. Depends only on matching definition.

**4.27: verified, with a small proof clarification.** Fix one player i and ONE distinguished a in A_i. Every matching contains exactly one profile with coordinate a. Its other coordinates have m^{n-1} choices. Deleting this profile and deleting each of its coordinate labels gives a bijection to matchings of the remaining n sets, all of size m-1. Conversely restore that profile. Residual label names do not affect the count, because coordinatewise relabellings induce bijections of matching sets. Thus the product formula follows. If one counted all a in A_i instead of fixing a one would introduce overcounting; the original proof is naturally read with a fixed a.

**4.28: verified.** Iterate 4.27 from 4.26: 2^{n-1} product_{k=3}^m k^{n-1}=(m!)^{n-1}. Independent direct proof: fix reference player 1; choose independently m! bijections A_1->A_j for j>1. Their graphs as a tuple yield exactly one matching, and every matching yields these maps uniquely. Formula extends to m=1 or n=1; unequal sizes give zero matchings. No substantive utility or group-theoretic dependency.

**5.1: verified.** Interpret 'subgroup of Gamma' as subgroup of Aut(Gamma), as explicitly defined. (ii)->(iii) follows from 4.23 with T=rho(G). Given (iii), choose reference B and coordinate bijections ell_i:A_i->B making matched coordinates equal. The game isomorphism f=(id;(ell_i)) to its relabelled payoff table conjugates M_pi to (pi;(id_B)); therefore T acts as payoff-preserving pure player permutations and transitivity makes the relabelled game standard symmetric. Conversely transport a transitive pure-permutation subgroup from any isomorphic standard symmetric game by conjugation; its player image is conjugated by the player permutation of the isomorphism, still transitive, and each player stabilizer has identity strategy component after transport. Thus (i)->(ii). Dependencies: 4.9, 4.23, label-dependent standard symmetry definition (transitive permutation invariance group). No 4.24 dependency.

**5.2: verified.** Same conjugation proof with T=S_N, since an n-transitive subgroup of S_N on n players is S_N (its action on ordered lists of all players has |S_N| distinct images). 4.23 then identifies G=M_{S_N}; relabelling makes all permutations exact automorphisms. No 4.24 dependency.

**5.3: verified.** By 5.1 choose M and player-transitive T with M_T<=Aut(Gamma). For i,j choose pi in T with pi(i)=j. For s in M, 4.22 gives M_pi(s)=s. Exact automorphism equality gives u_i(s)=u_j(M_pi s)=u_j(s). Original 'follows from 4.22' silently also uses transitivity, exact utility preservation and 5.1. This is not a statement about arbitrary symmetric games with nontrivial strategy-stabilizer action (e.g. Matching Pennies).

## Corrected 4.24, precise scope, fresh proof

Candidate correction within original finite nonempty n>=2 scope: the following are equivalent for arbitrary strategy cardinalities and arbitrary payoffs.

(a) Gamma is isomorphic to a game on a common strategy set B that is weakly anonymous: each player's payoff is invariant under all permutations fixing that player.

(b) There exists strategy-trivial G<=S_Gamma with rho(G)=S_N, and for every i and every g in G_i, u_i(gs)=u_i(s) for all profiles s.

(c) There is a matching M such that u_i(M_pi s)=u_i(s) whenever pi fixes i.

All three imply equal strategy cardinalities, so no separate equal-cardinality hypothesis is needed. If cardinalities differ all three are false: (a) bijections into common B require equality, (b) transitivity of rho(G) requires equality, and (c) matching projections require equality. No assumption on Aut(Gamma) belongs in (b).

Proof (b)->(c): rho(G)=S_N makes G player transitive; 4.23 gives G=M_{S_N}. Then G_i consists precisely of lifts of permutations fixing i, giving the required equalities.

Proof (c)->(b): take G=M_{S_N}; 4.20 gives a subgroup projecting identically onto S_N. A player stabilizer element has component M_ii=id at its fixed player, so G is strategy trivial. The stated payoff identities are exactly those of (c).

Proof (c)->(a): fix r, B=A_r, ell_i=M_ir and relabel using f=(id;(ell_i)); define v_i(t)=u_i(f^{-1}t). Then f M_pi f^{-1}=P_pi, by ell_{pi(i)} M_{i,pi(i)}=ell_i. Consequently for pi fixing i, v_i(P_pi t)=u_i(M_pi f^{-1}t)=v_i(t), as required. f is an exact game isomorphism by construction.

Proof (a)->(c): allow any player bijection sigma in the given isomorphism f:Gamma->Gamma'. Write f_i:A_i->B. Pull back the diagonal matching: M={s: f_i(s_i)=b for every i, for some b in B}. Its maps are M_ij=f_j^{-1}f_i. Directly f M_pi f^{-1}=P_{sigma pi sigma^{-1}}. If pi fixes i, sigma pi sigma^{-1} fixes sigma(i); use weak anonymity of Gamma' and the isomorphism payoff equation to get u_i(M_pi s)=u_i(s).

Distinct constants counterexample to original: n=2, A_1=A_2={0,1}, u_1(s)=0, u_2(s)=1. Weak anonymity holds because each player-fixing permutation group is trivial; diagonal M gives (iii). An exact automorphism swapping players would require 0=1, impossible. Thus Aut has trivial player image, so printed (ii) fails. The same construction works for every n>=2 with distinct player constants, since each own payoff is invariant under every profile bijection. Singleton sets do not rescue the printed claim: with all A_i singleton and distinct constants, (i),(iii) still hold, while no player-transitive automorphism group exists. Under the correction, M_{S_N} is a genuine n!-element group of game bijections, despite its trivial profile action in this singleton example.

'Weakest' action requirement: full player image in (b) can be replaced by the exact requirement that, after a coherent matching is chosen, the subgroups rho(G_i) generate every (S_N)_i; that requirement itself forces rho(G)=S_N for n>=3. More economical and useful is to dispense with G entirely and state (c), or to require invariance only for transpositions (jk) with i,j,k distinct, since these generate (S_N)_i. No transitivity/group assumption is needed in this direct matching formulation. For n=2 the transposition list is empty and weak anonymity is automatic on common-labelled games: equivalence reduces exactly to equal cardinalities. Merely replacing n-transitive by player transitive in (b) is NOT sufficient. For n=3 take equal binary labels and G generated by the pure 3-cycle. G is strategy trivial and regular on players so every G_i is identity, making the payoff condition vacuous. Set u_1(s)=s_2 with unequal dependence on the two opponents. No relabelling can make u_1 invariant under swapping opponents: one opponent genuinely changes u_1 and the other never does, and coordinate bijections/player renaming preserve that distinction. Thus (a),(c) fail. This also identifies why orbitwise matching extensions of 4.23 alone cannot weaken the anonymity condition.

## Downstream impact and limits

4.24 needs a changed subgroup domain and its own proof; keeping G<=Aut would instead characterize fully symmetric games, because n-transitive strategy-trivial automorphism groups are exactly 5.2(ii), and the extra stabilizer payoff condition is then automatic. Therefore original 4.24 accidentally equates weak anonymity up to relabelling with full symmetry.

4.19 needs player-indexed objects/arrows to survive equal or overlapping strategy labels. 4.20 onward can be justified directly from coordinate projections and are not invalidated by this presentation defect. 4.10 needs explicit 'bijection of hom-sets' language; these are torsors rather than naturally groups.
