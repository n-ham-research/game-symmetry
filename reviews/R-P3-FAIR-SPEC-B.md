**Verdict: the mathematical claims pass this targeted review.**

- F1 quantifies over a fixed whole-experiment typed transport g, fixed focal
  i, every focal action and every product opponent law. Dirac opponent laws
  prove necessity; finite summation proves sufficiency. The full-support
  version follows by approximation, including singleton strategy boundaries.
  Simultaneity over every focal i is correctly required before inferring one
  exact automorphism. Selected laws or separate focal identifications would
  not suffice.
- F2's successor graph has indegrees 2,1,0. Positive own-action differences
  force all component maps to preserve binary labels, after which an exact
  automorphism must commute with that graph. The player image is therefore
  trivial despite separate focal opportunity equivalences. The optimized-value
  example has both upper and lower security values 1 but distinct attainable
  payoff ranges, excluding a role swap.
- F3's histogram factorization and orbit-average characterization follow from
  opponent permutation orbits. Hidden information is correctly distinguished
  from ex post indifference. The displayed cyclic example averages to the
  focal action and fails opponent anonymity at the stated profiles.
- F4's uniform expectations equal 3/2; own-action magnitudes 2 and 1 exclude
  role-swapping exact symmetry. The symmetric own-action game with a biased
  selection rule correctly refutes untransported expected-equality claims.
- F5's change-of-variables argument applies to coordinated coalition laws on
  the two explicit simplexes. In its counterexample, a coalition containing
  both or neither of players 0 and 1 has zero sum payoff. If it contains
  exactly one, its payoff is that player's bit minus the outsider's bit.
  Each side choosing its relevant bit 1 gives lower/upper bound 0, hence value
  0. The constant third utility excludes exact player transitivity.
- S1's hom-set bijection, inverse, free/transitive commuting actions and
  choice-dependent group law all type-check. S2 correctly distinguishes exact,
  pure ordinal and independent-mixed quantifiers. Both counterexamples check;
  the affine characterization is explicitly reserved for all profile
  lotteries. S3's measurability, finite positive area and n>=2 qualification
  suffice for its limited geometric conclusions.
